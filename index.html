How this works

Syrup: equal parts sugar and water by cup, then reduced for the volume sugar loses when it dissolves — syrup = (sugar + water) × reduction factor.

Finished food: the syrup plus three more parts water (three times the water that went into the syrup) — food = syrup + 3 × water‑for‑syrup. Because that dilution water is sized off the syrup's own water content, not its shrunken volume, the final ratio always lands at 1 part sugar to 4 parts water, no matter what reduction factor you use.

Leftover syrup: back-solves the sugar that must be in a jar of syrup of a given size, then sizes the dilution water the same way.
